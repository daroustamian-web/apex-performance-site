**When you lose fat, approximately 84% of it leaves your body through your lungs.**

You literally breathe it out.

The remaining portion leaves as water through sweat, urine, and other bodily processes.

The body doesn't simply "burn" fat and make it disappear.

Your breath.

## The Gym Is Responsible for Less Than You Think

Here's another fact that surprises almost everyone.

Your workout is only a small percentage of your total daily calorie burn.

The average breakdown looks something like this:

* **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): 60-70%**

   — simply staying alive.
* **Thermic Effect of Food: 10-15%**

   — digesting and processing meals.
* **Exercise Activity: Approximately 5%**

   — your actual workout.
* **NEAT (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis): 15-30%**

   — everything else you do throughout the day.

Read that again.

The workout most people obsess over often accounts for only about **5% of total daily energy expenditure.**

Five percent.

Meanwhile, the largest contributor is the body's ability to maintain itself, regulate itself, and remain active throughout the remaining twenty-three hours of the day.

Even more fascinating, research shows that NEAT can vary by as much as **2,000 calories per day between individuals.**

Two people can eat the exact same meals.

Follow the exact same workout program.

Spend the same hour in the gym.

Yet one can burn nearly 2,000 more calories simply because they move more throughout the day.

Walking.

Standing.

Taking stairs.

Fidgeting.

Living.

This is why increasing daily movement from 3,000 steps to 10,000 steps often creates more fat-loss momentum than adding another brutal workout.

The gym builds muscle.

Daily movement burns fat.

And recovery determines whether your body has the energy to sustain both.
